I am an evolutionary and computational biologist, currently working as a contractor at the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History. My current work focuses on establishing, maintaining, and growing a centralized collection of freshwater mussel genetic materials in collaboration with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. I also contribute to research projects in freshwater mussel genomics. My previous research has focused on reproductive and viral evolution, using molecular, genomic, and computational approaches.

I received my PhD in Biology from Simon Fraser University, where I worked with Mike Hart on evolutionary genomics and the evolution of reproductive genes. Before that, I completed an MSc in Biology with a concentration in Ecology, Evolution, and Conservation Biology at San Francisco State University and a bachelor's degree in Biology at Humboldt State University.

I was born in Peru and moved to the United States as a teenager.

Outside of science, I spend a lot of time exploring the world with our young daughter, while trying to make room for some things of my own—getting back on the water and figuring out what will grow in our shady native garden.

You can read about some of my projects, or find a more complete record of my research on my Google Scholar profile.
